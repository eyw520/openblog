Every example in a new tool is about the tool. That gets tiresome, and it hides the thing you actually want to see, which is what a normal piece of writing looks like once it is set in type.

So this post is about nothing in particular.

A notebook is not a record of what happened. It is a record of what you noticed, which is a smaller and stranger set. The distinction matters because the second kind is the only one worth keeping — anyone can reconstruct a day from a calendar, but nobody can reconstruct the way the light came through the window of a train somewhere north of the city, or the fact that you thought about it for the rest of the afternoon.

> The impulse to write things down is a peculiarly compulsive one, inexplicable to those who do not share it.

Notice what the type is doing here. Quotations sit slightly apart with a rule in the accent color. Links, like [the archive](/writing), are underlined rather than only colored, so they remain visible to readers who cannot distinguish the hue.

## Lists behave themselves

- Items sit on a comfortable measure, never running the full width of a wide screen.
- The line length is capped deliberately, because long lines are hard to track back from.
- The spacing between them is inherited from one place, so it stays consistent everywhere.

Ordinary text resumes here, and the rhythm holds. That is the whole intention: you should be able to write without thinking about the design, and have it come out looking considered anyway.
